A word on roles

Roles are held by an account, and one account can hold several. An instructor who also administers the database holds both roles and sees both doors. Nothing in this guide assumes you hold only one.

1Everyone — getting in

There is nothing to install and no password to remember. You need the address your detachment uses, and the Google account it has on file for you.

The join link

Most people never type the address at all. A join link carries the detachment's configuration inside it, so tapping one sets your device up and signs you in.

Or a QR code on a projector

An administrator can put the same link on screen as a QR code and point a room at it — the fastest way to enrol a whole flight at once. Chapter 6 covers producing one.

Signing in

9ThirtyOne issues no passwords of its own. You sign in with Google, and whether you get in is decided by your detachment's roster — a list an administrator keeps. An address that is not on it is turned away, however valid the Google account.

Use the address your detachment has for you

If sign-in refuses you, the usual cause is signing in with a different Google account from the one on the roster — a personal address where the detachment listed a school one, or the other way round. Ask your administrator which address they hold.

The home screen

Home shows the doors you can open. It is deliberately spare: on a shared detachment laptop this is the screen someone walks up to.

Settings, and installing it on a phone

Settings holds appearance, accessibility and the database location. Every role has it.

On a phone, use your browser's Add to Home Screen to install 9ThirtyOne as an app. It then opens full screen, works offline, and keeps your session between uses.

2Cadet

Your side of the app is small on purpose: see what is assigned to you, answer it, and know what happened to your answer.

Finding your feedback

The cadet screen lists the feedback requests assigned to you — what is open, when it is due, and what you have already submitted. Nobody else's work appears here, and you cannot see other cadets' answers.

Answering

Open a request and answer it. Two things are worth knowing before you start.

  • You choose a word, not a number. Ratings are anchored words — Detrimental through Outstanding — and the arithmetic runs on the number underneath. You never pick a digit.
  • You get one attempt. A second try at the same request is refused at the form, so nothing is double-counted.

What happens to your answer

Your answer goes to a small script running inside your detachment's own Google account, which checks who you are and writes the file. You are never asked for access to your own Google Drive, and you never get access to the detachment's folder.

On an anonymous form, two separate files are written: the response, which carries no name, and the receipt, which records that you took part. Keeping them apart is what lets the detachment chase the people who still owe feedback without attaching anyone's words to their name.

Anonymous results are withheld below three responses

Not "enough", not "several" — three. Below that line, only the count of who has taken part is shown, so a single answer cannot be picked out by elimination.

What anonymity cannot do

Removing names cannot change what you wrote. An answer describing a role only one person holds, or an incident only one person witnessed, identifies its author whatever the system stored. Treat anonymous feedback as feedback, not as statistics.

This is not a reporting channel

Written answers are screened for wording that may need a person's attention, and a match is put in front of cadre quickly. But the screen matches words, never meaning, and nobody is monitoring submissions in real time. Do not report a crime, an assault, a threat or a safety emergency here. If someone is in danger, contact emergency services; use your detachment's designated channels for everything else.

3Instructor

The Instructor Panel is where a detachment's ordinary feedback work happens: build a form, issue it, read what comes back, and keep the record.

The panel, and its tabs

Everything lives behind five tabs. The panel opens on what needs attention rather than on a list to work through.

Feedback requestsWhat has been issued, to whom, open and due dates, and how many have answered.
Responses & analysisWhat came back, analysed — distributions, splits, written answers.

CadetsWho is on the roster for the classes you work with, and what they still owe.

Creating a form and issuing it

A form is a reusable set of questions. A feedback request is that form issued to a class, an event, or named cadets, with an open date and a due date. Separating the two is what stops the wording drifting term to term: save a form as a template, and next term starts from the same questions.

Reading what comes back

Ratings are analysed rather than stacked. The panel reports a distribution, names the questions where the room genuinely split, and flags a lone dissenting view instead of letting an average bury it.

Why the average is not the headline

An average is the one number that can be true of a group and describe none of its members. Where a set is polarised, the panel names the split and shows both groups.

Written answers, and the safety screen

Written answers are grouped and searchable, and every one is screened against word lists for hazing, harassment, discrimination, violence, self-harm, substance misuse and integrity concerns. A match is put in front of cadre quickly.

A match is not a finding, and a clear screen is not proof

The screen matches words, never meaning. It cannot tell "we discussed hazing prevention" from "I was hazed", it raises some matches that are nothing, and a clear screen is never proof that nothing was reported. It exists to get a real disclosure in front of a person faster — never to decide whether one happened. A match means read that answer now, and follow the detachment's own reporting procedures.

Flagged feedback cannot be deleted from this panel

If a response contains language the safety screen flagged, an instructor cannot remove it. A database administrator can, and the deletion is recorded against their name. If you are the subject of the feedback, refer it rather than handling it yourself.

Chasing people without unmasking them

Because taking part is recorded in a different file from what was said, the panel can tell you that two cadets still owe feedback on an anonymous request without telling you what anyone wrote. Use the Cadets tab to see who is outstanding.

By instructor — your own results

This tab answers "how am I doing?" by gathering feedback about a person rather than about a form. As an instructor you see your own results here.

What this view is, stated precisely

By instructor narrows to the subjects your role oversees. It is not a confidentiality boundary between instructors: the detachment's shared feedback remains readable to instructors through Responses & analysis. Treat it as a lens that gathers results by subject, not as a guarantee that colleagues cannot see each other's.

4Cadre

Cadre hold everything an instructor holds, plus a second panel pointed at folders instructors cannot open at all.

Two panels, one interface

The Cadre Panel is the Instructor Panel — same tabs, same filters, same form creator. The only difference is which folder the records come out of. That is deliberate: a cadre member should not have to learn a second interface to do the same job with restricted material.

Separate folders, not a badge on a list

Restricted feedback is not an item in the instructor list wearing a lock icon — it is in a different folder, and the server decides who may read that folder at all. So "locked" means locked, and the ordinary detachment list never contains anything a cadet could not see. It also means you can read a list on a shared screen without checking who is behind you.

Issuing feedback into the cadre space

When you create a request from the Cadre Panel, it is issued into the cadre space and its responses land there. Cadets answer exactly as they always do; they are not told which space the request belongs to.

Overseeing instructors

In By instructor, cadre see the instructors they oversee, and themselves — where an instructor sees only their own results.

5Commander

A commander holds the cadre view plus two things of their own: a private area, and sight of everyone.

Your own area

The commander space is reached through the Cadre Panel, in the same space picker. Feedback issued there is readable only by the commander — cadre included in the exclusion.

Everyone, cadre included

In By instructor a commander sees every subject — instructors and cadre alike. This is the view that answers "how is the cadre doing?", which no other role can ask.

The tiers are narrowed on the server

The submission server applies this rule itself, so the app will not serve an instructor a By-instructor view of somebody else. On a detachment running without that server there is nothing to enforce it, and the tiers are a lens in the browser only.

6Database administrator

The administrator keeps the roster, gets people in, and looks after the folder. It is a separate role from instructor — holding it does not open the panels.

The roster is who may sign in

There is no password list. The roster decides who may sign in and as what, keyed on the Google address each person will actually use.

  • Use the address they will sign in with. A school address and the Gmail account behind it are two different strings; only the one on the roster works.
  • Changing an email keeps their history. Receipts are filed under a stable username, so correcting an address does not orphan what they have already submitted.
  • Deactivate rather than delete when someone leaves. The record of their participation stays intact.
  • Keep at least two administrators. Losing a single account should not lock the detachment out of its own folder.

Getting everyone in

Produce a join link, or put it on a projector as a QR code and enrol a room in one go.

The link is configuration, not a secret

It carries the Client ID, the folder and the server address — all of which are designed to be public. It grants nothing on its own: access is still decided by the roster, so someone who is not on it gets no further than the sign-in screen.

Backups, exports and the activity log

  • Export a backup regularly from the Database tab, and keep a copy off-site.
  • The anonymised export strips names for a copy you keep outside the folder. What it cannot strip is what somebody wrote — see the limit stated in chapter 2.
  • The activity log records deletions and roster changes with who did them. Nothing in the app can delete an entry from it.
  • Maintenance runs from the owner's device. Backup, restore, wipe and reindex act on the whole folder, and the submission server deliberately offers no way to do any of them remotely — an address that could empty a detachment's records on request is not one worth having.

7What each role cannot reach

Access is decided by the role your account holds. A door you cannot open is refused outright — not hidden, and not merely absent from a menu.

Administering the database is not instructing

The last row surprises people. An account holding only the database administrator role manages the roster and the folder, and cannot open the panels. Someone who does both jobs holds both roles, and then sees both doors. That is the point of separating them: managing accounts should not require the ability to read everyone's feedback.
