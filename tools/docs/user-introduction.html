Why it exists, and how it works

Why

A detachment serves cadets from several Crosstown universities, so it cannot run on any one university's IT system. It stands up a Det Google Account for mail — and that account arrives with no course platform, no survey tool, and nowhere for evaluations to live.

Leadership Laboratory has no feedback capture at all, and LLAB is where cadets lead cadets: the part of the program most worth measuring and the part with the least instrumentation.

So upperclassmen improvise in consumer form tools. It works, sometimes well — and it has no oversight, no archive, and no continuity past the term that cadet graduates. That is a records and oversight gap that a tooling gap produced.

How

A web page, not an installation. A browser opens it; a phone can add it to the home screen. Nothing to install, no server to maintain, no account with anyone.

The database is a Drive folder in the Det Google Account. Forms, responses, receipts, the roster and the activity log are ordinary files, readable in Drive with no special software. If the detachment stopped using 9ThirtyOne tomorrow, every response would still be sitting there.

The app can only reach files it made

It asks Google for drive.file — files this app created, and nothing else in that Drive. The rest was never granted, not merely withheld.

For instructors and cadet instructors

Build a form, issue it, read what comes back

  • A form is reusable. Build a set of questions once, save it as a template, and the wording stops drifting term to term.
  • Issue it as a feedback request to a class, an event, or named cadets, with an open date and a due date.
  • Ratings are analysed, not stacked. Distributions rather than averages, and a question where the room genuinely split is called out instead of averaged into the middle.
  • Written answers are grouped and searchable, and every one is screened for safety concerns so a match reaches cadre quickly.
  • Nothing important sits unread. The panel opens on what needs attention rather than on a list to work through.

For cadre and commanders

The same screen, pointed at folders instructors cannot open

  • The Cadre Panel is the Instructor Panel. Same tabs, same filters, same form creator — the difference is which folder the records come out of, so there is no second interface to learn.
  • Cadre material is a different folder, not a badge. "Locked" means locked: the server decides what an account may read at all, so the detachment list never contains anything a cadet could not see.
  • Commanders reach their own area in the same panel, for feedback only they can read.
  • By‑instructor answers "how is this instructor doing?" — an instructor sees their own results, cadre see the instructors they oversee, and a commander sees everyone, cadre included.
What By-instructor is

A view that narrows to the subjects your role oversees. It is not a confidentiality boundary between instructors — the panel's own Responses & analysis still shows the detachment's shared feedback to any instructor.

For cadets

Only what is assigned to you, and once each

  • Getting in is a link. Tap a join link or scan a QR code from a projector, sign in with the Google account the detachment already mails you at, and you are in — nothing to configure, nothing to paste.
  • Your screen lists your requests — what is open, what is due, and what you have already submitted. Nobody else's work appears there.
  • You choose a word, not a number. The scale is worded; the maths runs on the number underneath it.
  • Once each. A second attempt at the same request is refused at the form, so a busy room cannot double-count anyone.
  • It works offline. An answer written with no signal is held on the device and sent when the connection returns.
A cadet is never asked for Drive access

Sign-in identifies you. It grants this app nothing in your own Google Drive, and gives you no access to the detachment's folder.

9ThirtyOne — User Introduction5
For cadets

Answering, on the walk out of the classroom

  • Built for a phone first. The scale is thumb-sized and anchored, so a rating is one tap rather than a slider fought with.
  • Written answers are optional unless the instructor marked them required, and they are where the useful detail lives.
  • You get a receipt — the record that you took part. On an anonymous form that receipt is stored in a different file from your answer, which is what lets the detachment chase the people who still owe feedback without attaching anyone's words to their name.
Anonymous, and what it cannot do

Removing names cannot change what you wrote. An answer describing a role only one person holds, or an incident only one person witnessed, identifies its author whatever the system stored. Treat anonymous feedback as feedback, not as statistics.

What holds, and what it cannot do

The guarantees

Nothing reaches the publisher

No server of theirs, no database of theirs, no account with them.

Anonymous results are withheld below three responses

Not "enough", not "several" — three. Below that line only the count of who took part is shown, so a single answer cannot be picked out by elimination.

Taking part is recorded apart from what was said

Which is what lets the app say two cadets still owe feedback without attaching anyone's answers to their name.

And the limits that keep them honest

Removing names cannot change what someone wrote

An answer describing a role only one person holds identifies its author no matter what fields were stored.

The safety screen matches words, never meaning

It cannot read context, sarcasm or quotation. It raises some matches that are nothing, and a clear screen is never proof that nothing was reported. It exists to get a real disclosure in front of a person faster — never to decide whether one happened.

Not an official system, and not a reporting channel

Nothing here is an official record. If something requires official reporting, use the detachment's designated channels. If someone is in danger, contact emergency services.
